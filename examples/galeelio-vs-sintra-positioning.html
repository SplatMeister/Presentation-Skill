Twelve employees who don't talk to each other isn't a team.

The complaint

Independent reviews converge on the same issue, again and again: the helpers don't share context. A business owner has to manually carry information from one AI employee's chat window to the next.

In practice

Cassie can't hand a qualified lead to Milli. Dexter's analysis doesn't automatically inform Penn's next piece of copy. Every "employee" works in its own silo.

06 / 17
Shallow automation

Falls short of tools like Zapier — no complex workflows or conditional triggers.

A fixed roster

Little ability to customize the twelve helpers, or build one of your own.

Pricing that climbs

No true free plan — a buyer has to commit financially before testing it properly.

One buyer only

Built for the solopreneur — no real governance, permissions, or compliance story for mid-sized teams.

None of this makes Sintra a bad product. It makes it a product with a shape — built for one kind of customer, doing one kind of job well.

An AI workforce should behave like a real team, not a directory of specialists.

The mechanism

Where Sintra sells twelve employees and leaves coordination to you, Galeelio sells a squad — agents pre-configured for a specific job, wired together so one agent's output becomes the next agent's input. No copying between windows.
